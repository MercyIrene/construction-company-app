# 01 · Business Strategy

## 1. Market

### 1.1 Structure of Kenyan residential construction

Kenyan housing is produced through four channels:

1. **Individual self-build (our market):** a household acquires land (often incrementally, via sacco/chama or inheritance), then commissions design and construction directly — hiring an architect or draughtsman, a contractor or fundis, and buying materials themselves. This is the dominant channel for formal owner-occupied homes outside estates. Projects run KES 3M (modest bungalow) to KES 80M+ (high-end villa), typically 8–24 months, frequently in stop-start phases matched to cash availability.
2. **Developer estates:** built-for-sale units, concentrated in Nairobi/Mombasa satellites. Not our market (developers have their own PM), though estate buyers become Home OS customers later.
3. **Incremental informal:** room-by-room construction, largely unpermitted. Below our initial price floor; long-term relevant via lightweight products.
4. **Affordable housing programme (government):** institutional; not our market.

### 1.2 Sizing (assumption-flagged; validation in doc 03)

Working top-down estimate — to be replaced with bottom-up data in the validation sprint:

- Formal + semi-formal individually-commissioned homes started per year in Kenya: **~50,000–80,000** (derived from cement consumption trends, county approval volumes, and NCA project registrations; wide error bars — flagged as assumption **A9**).
- Of these, projects ≥ KES 5M with a remote or time-poor owner (our serviceable segment): assume **15–20%** → **~8,000–15,000 projects/yr**, ~KES 100–200B annual construction spend.
- Serviceable obtainable in 5 years at 2–3% of segment: **~250–400 projects/yr**, KES 4–6B under management, revenue KES 300–450M/yr from delivery fees alone before network/materials/finance lines.

The strategic point does not depend on precision: even pessimistic reads leave a venture-scale wedge market, and Phases 2–3 expand the addressable set (smaller projects, renovations, maintenance, then East Africa — Uganda and Tanzania replicate the self-build + diaspora pattern).

### 1.3 The customer's alternatives today

| Alternative | Why it fails them |
|---|---|
| Relative/friend as supervisor | Unqualified, conflicted, and the #1 source of heartbreak stories |
| Fundi-led ("we'll manage it") | No independent verification; incentive to inflate materials |
| Hiring an architect for "full supervision" | Good architects do this well but visit fortnightly at best, don't control money flow, and are unaffordable/unavailable below the high end |
| Traditional PM firms | Serve commercial and KES 100M+ residential; no productized offer at KES 5–50M |
| Flying home repeatedly | Costs thousands of dollars, still can't verify quality, destroys careers |

**Insight:** the competition is not another platform. It is *the relative with the M-Pesa till number*. Our marketing job is giving people social permission to institutionalize what family was failing to do.

## 2. Segments and beachhead

Priority order:

1. **Diaspora builders (beachhead A):** US/UK/Gulf-based Kenyans, project budgets KES 8–40M. Maximum pain, USD income, pay premiums for certainty, dense referral networks. Buying trigger: land already owned; a previous bad experience (theirs or a relative's).
2. **Urban professional remote builders (beachhead B):** Nairobi/Mombasa professionals building in home counties or satellite towns. Same structural problem, smaller budgets, easier ops (reachable sites), and they seed the mass market.
3. **Later:** local time-poor builders; renovators; small landlords (rental units are a huge self-build category with a rational ROI mindset); estate buyers (Home OS only).

We deliberately serve **two beachheads with one operation**: diaspora projects fund premium unit economics; Nairobi-metro projects keep field ops dense and cheap. Both build in the same counties.

## 3. Positioning

**Category:** we are creating one — "verified home construction delivery." We refuse the "Uber for fundis" frame (commoditizes trust) and the "construction marketplace" frame (we are accountable for outcomes, not introductions).

**Promise:** *Your home, built as designed, on budget you can see, with proof at every step — or your money doesn't move.*

**Against:**
- vs. relative/fundi supervision → independence, qualifications, money control, evidence.
- vs. architect full-supervision → continuous (not fortnightly) presence, financial controls, one accountable party, tech-native reporting.
- vs. PM firms → productized, priced for KES 5–50M, consumer-grade experience.
- vs. materials/fundi apps → we own the outcome, not a transaction.

## 4. Competitive landscape

Categories, not exhaustive names (landscape re-scan is validation task V12):

- **Materials marketplaces** (e.g., Jumba and similar B2B building-material platforms): solve procurement, not trust/delivery. Potential partners in Phase 2.
- **Artisan-matching apps** (fundi directories/booking apps): solve small jobs; trust model too thin for whole-home builds. Different market.
- **Traditional PM/QS firms and design-build contractors:** real competitors at the top end; not productized, don't scale down, no data layer. Some become network professionals on our platform.
- **Global self-build finance/PM platforms** (e.g., iBUILD-style): distribution and localization gaps; watch closely.
- **Banks/saccos with construction products:** control money but have no delivery capability — prime partnership targets (construction mortgage disbursement against our verified milestones is a bank's dream).

**Structural read:** everyone else is attacking a *transaction* (materials, labour, listing). Nobody owns the *project* and its money flow. That is the position we take, and it is operationally hard enough to be defensible.

## 5. Moat construction (sequenced)

1. **Now → 18mo — Operational excellence + brand:** delivery playbooks, evidence standards, escrow mechanics; become the name whispered in diaspora groups.
2. **12 → 36mo — Proprietary performance data:** every verified milestone generates labelled data on contractor timeliness, budget variance, defect rates. The **Reliability Score** becomes the credential contractors defend — and defend by staying on-platform.
3. **24mo+ — Home File switching costs:** the as-built record, warranties, and maintenance history make Msingi the default for every subsequent shilling spent on the home.
4. **Throughout — Network effects (moderate, real):** more verified projects → better pros join to access decision-ready demand → better outcomes → more referrals. Not winner-take-all, but strong regional density economics: inspection routes, supplier terms, and county-approval expertise all compound locally. **Depth before breadth** — dominate 4 counties before opening the 5th.

## 6. Changes we made to the founding brief, with reasoning

| Brief said | We build instead | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Platform/marketplace from day one | Managed service first, marketplace at Phase 2 | Cold start: a marketplace with 0 verified pros and 0 track record asks users to trust exactly what they distrust. Doing delivery ourselves creates the data and playbooks the marketplace needs; the service revenue funds it. |
| Journey starts at architect matching | Journey starts wherever the customer is; wedge is money+verification | ~Half of prospects arrive with drawings already in hand (validation A4). Matching is an entry door, not the engine. Building matching-first over-invests in the lowest-pain step. |
| Platform "coordinates construction" | Owner's representative model, professionals contract directly with owner | Taking a GC position means NCA contractor licensure, defects liability, working-capital exposure, and an incentive conflict (we'd profit from cost growth). The owner's-rep position keeps incentives clean: we are paid to protect the owner. |
| "Eventually the OS" as vision | Home File designed into the domain model on day one | Retrofitting a system of record is nearly impossible; capturing it as a by-product of delivery is nearly free. |
| Comprehensive design scope up front | Standardized scope templates + progressive elaboration | Full every-discipline design before budget-setting front-loads cost and kills conversion. We stage: feasibility estimate → concept design → full documentation, with decision gates. |

## 7. Strategic risks & posture

- **Regulatory drift** (e.g., NCA expanding PM licensing): we pre-emptively register the entity and delivery leads with relevant bodies and keep the owner's-rep contract structure adaptable (doc 06).
- **Macroeconomic shocks** (FX, cement prices): milestone contracts carry indexed materials provisions; diaspora USD income is partially counter-cyclical to KES.
- **County-level political friction:** permits are county turf; we hire locally-connected compliance associates per county and never pay bribes — the brand *is* the anti-corruption promise (Ethics policy, doc 07 §7).
- **Founder/team gap:** the model requires construction-operations credibility. First three hires include a licensed architect or QS as Head of Delivery (doc 07 §3).
