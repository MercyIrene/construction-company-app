# Executive Summary

**Company (working name):** Msingi Build Technologies Ltd ("Msingi")
**HQ:** Nairobi, Kenya · **Date:** 2026-07-07 · **Stage:** pre-seed

---

## 1. The problem

Kenya builds most of its homes one family at a time. Individually commissioned homes on family-owned land — not developer estates — are the dominant mode of formal residential construction, and the process is broken in a specific, expensive way:

- **Money leaks.** The homeowner hands cash to a relative, a fundi, or a contractor and hopes. Diverted funds, inflated material invoices, ghost purchases, and abandoned sites are so common that "diaspora construction heartbreak" is a cultural trope. Losses of 20–40% of budget are routinely reported anecdotally; even successful projects overrun.
- **Progress is unverifiable.** The owner is in Nairobi, London, or Dallas; the site is in Kitengela, Kisii, or Nyeri. There is no trustworthy, independent account of what physically exists on site versus what has been paid for.
- **Professionals are unverifiable.** NCA, BORAQS and EBK registers prove licensure, not performance. Referrals prove one data point. There is no track record infrastructure.
- **Coordination fails.** Sequencing, permits, inspections, and inter-trade dependencies are managed informally, causing rework, delay, and compliance exposure.

The result: a market with enormous spend, chronically low trust, and no system of record.

## 2. The company we are building

Msingi is the **trust and delivery layer for self-build residential construction**, launched as a managed service and evolving into the market's operating system:

- **Phase 1 — Verified Delivery (the wedge).** Msingi acts as the homeowner's professional representative. We vet and assemble the project team (architect, QS, engineer, contractor), put the project on standard contracts, place funds under milestone-locked disbursement control, and verify every milestone with geotagged, timestamped photo/video evidence and physical inspection before money moves. The homeowner sees everything in a real-time portal; the site team runs on our field tools.
- **Phase 2 — Network.** With performance data accumulating, we open the vetted professional network with matching, standardized bidding on QS-produced bills of quantities, and a contractor **Reliability Score** — the industry's first performance-based credential.
- **Phase 3 — Home OS.** Materials procurement at negotiated rates, financing partnerships, insurance, and the **Home File**: the permanent digital record (designs, as-builts, permits, warranties, maintenance) that stays with the home and powers a maintenance and renovation relationship for decades.

## 3. Beachhead customer

**Remote builders**: diaspora Kenyans and urban professionals building homes (KES 5M–50M construction value) on land they own in the Nairobi metro counties (Kiambu, Kajiado, Machakos) and selected upcountry corridors. They have money, acute distrust born of experience, and no ability to supervise. They already pay informal "supervisors" who are part of the problem. They are reachable through dense, high-trust channels: diaspora WhatsApp/Facebook groups, saccos, churches, and employer networks.

## 4. Business model (detail: doc 02)

| Line | Mechanism | Phase |
|---|---|---|
| Delivery management fee | 5–8% of construction value, milestone-billed | 1 |
| Project setup fee | Fixed fee: feasibility, team assembly, contracts, permits | 1 |
| Professional network fees | Success fee on matches; SaaS tools for pros | 2 |
| Materials margin | 2–5% on procured materials via negotiated supplier rates | 2–3 |
| Financial services | Escrow/float income, insurance and financing referral | 2–3 |
| Home OS subscription | Maintenance plans, Home File, renovation projects | 3 |

Representative unit: a KES 15M home at a 6.5% delivery fee + KES 150K setup ≈ **KES 1.125M (~USD 8.7K) revenue per project** over 12–18 months, with one delivery manager carrying 8–12 concurrent projects. Gross margin at maturity ~55–65% on the service line; software layers are higher.

## 5. Why now

- Smartphone + M-Pesa ubiquity makes evidence capture, remote approval, and controlled disbursement practical at fundi level.
- eCitizen-era digitization of permits and registries (NCA, county e-permitting) makes compliance workflows automatable.
- Diaspora remittances (>USD 4B/yr, housing a leading use) keep growing while trust mechanisms haven't.
- No incumbent owns this: existing plays are materials marketplaces, fundi-matching apps, or traditional PM firms serving the top end. The trust + delivery + data position is open.

## 6. The moat (in order of durability)

1. **Verified performance data** on professionals — compounds with every project; impossible to copy without doing the projects.
2. **The Home File** — switching cost that grows for decades; the beachhead for maintenance revenue.
3. **Process & playbooks** — codified delivery operations that make quality repeatable at marketplace scale.
4. **Brand as the trust mark** — in a referral market, the first trusted institutional brand wins referrals structurally.

## 7. The plan

- **Months 0–6 (Validate):** 10–15 concierge projects in Nairobi metro run on a thin software spine (client portal + field evidence capture + disbursement workflow). Kill/confirm the assumptions register (doc 03).
- **Months 6–18 (Systematize):** Full Phase-1 product; 40–60 active projects; delivery playbooks v2; NPS > 60; evidence-verified disbursement on 100% of milestones.
- **Months 18–36 (Open the network):** Marketplace mechanics, Reliability Score public, materials procurement, first financing partnership; 250+ projects/yr; expand corridors.
- **Beyond:** East Africa expansion (Uganda, Tanzania, Rwanda) replaying the same wedge.

**Funding:** ~USD 1.5M pre-seed/seed for 24 months to the Systematize milestones (doc 02 §6).

## 8. What could kill us, and our answer

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Customers won't pay 6%+ for supervision | They already pay 10%+ in leakage and informal supervision; pricing is framed against loss, not against "free". Validated in first 90 days (doc 03, A1). |
| Contractors reject oversight | We bring them a pipeline of well-funded, decision-ready clients and on-time payment certainty — the thing they lack most. Probation tiers cap our exposure (doc 05). |
| Handling client money creates regulatory/fiduciary exposure | We never take custody: dual-mandate project accounts with a partner bank; platform holds instruction rights, not funds (doc 05 §5, ADR-0007). |
| A well-funded competitor copies the model | Speed to performance-data scale + brand in tight-knit diaspora channels; the model is operationally hard, which is a feature. |
| Ops don't scale (service business trap) | Software-first ops design from day one; delivery manager load ratio is a tracked KPI with a floor; marketplace phase shifts execution to the network. |
